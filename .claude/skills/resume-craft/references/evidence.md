# Evidence behind the resume-craft rules

Researched 2026-11-22. "Read" = the primary text was fetched and read. "Search summary" = only a search-tool summary of the page was seen (treat as secondary). Re-verify anything marked unverified before quoting it to anyone.

## Primary sources (read in full)

| Source | What it establishes | Date / caveat |
|---|---|---|
| **Greenhouse Support — Unsuccessful resume parse**<br>https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse | Greenhouse "scans an imported resume and auto-fills appropriate fields". Parse can't handle files > 2.5 MB. Causes of unsuccessful parse listed: spaces between letters; graphics/photos/word art; image uploads; complex resumes with tables, headers, footers; name/contact in header, footer or text box; columned layout; no clear sections / differing formats within sections; company names without identifying words (Inc., Co., LTD, LLC); incomplete job titles ("Sr. Account Exec" instead of "Senior Account Executive"). Also: fake/placeholder data is skipped. Says failures cause "a partial resume parse that will need to be manually corrected". | Updated 2026-03-02. One vendor (Greenhouse). Says nothing about keyword scoring or auto-reject. |
| **Greenhouse Support — Supported formats**<br>https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/360052218132-Supported-formats-for-resumes-cover-letters-and-other-candidate-uploads | Accepted uploads: .doc .docx .pdf .rtf .txt; up to 100 MB. | Updated 2026-08-19. |
| **TheLadders eye-tracking study (Will Evans, 2012)**<br>https://www.bu.edu/com/files/2018/10/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf | 30 professional recruiters, 10 weeks. ~6 seconds on the initial fit/no-fit decision (self-reports had suggested 4–5 minutes). ~80% of review time on: name, current title/company, previous title/company, previous and current position start/end dates, education. Beyond those, recruiters "scan for keywords". Resumes with an obvious information hierarchy rated 6.2/7 for usability vs 3.9 before a professional rewrite; gaze path erratic and cognitive load higher on poorly organised resumes. | Commercial sponsor (a job board selling resume rewriting); small sample; 2012. A 2018 follow-up reported 7.4 s (HR Dive, search summary). |
| **Harvard College (Mignone Center) — Guide to Creating a Strong Resume**<br>https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume | Language: specific, active, "written to express not impress", fact-based (quantify and qualify), "written for people who / systems that scan quickly". Top mistakes: spelling/grammar, missing email/phone, passive language, not organised/concise/easy to skim, not demonstrating results. Don't: personal pronouns, abbreviate, narrative style, slang, picture, age/gender, references, start each line with a date. Do: consistent format, white space, headings in order of importance, reverse-chronological, no gaps, "be sure that your formatting will translate properly if converted to a .pdf". Action-verb lists by category. Says generative AI shouldn't be the primary author because output is generic. | Written for students/early-career; one-page norm there. Official but not ATS-specific. |
| **HBS Managing the Future of Work — Hidden Workers: Untapped Talent**<br>https://www.hbs.edu/managing-the-future-of-work/research/hidden-workers-untapped-talent | Abstract only: hiring processes designed to find "perfect" candidates "systematically exclude several categories of qualified workers". | The full report PDF is on a host not reachable here, so the widely quoted "88% of employers" figure is **unverified** — don't cite it. |

## Our own test (2026-11-22): the Everstage PDF
Plain-text extraction (`pdftotext`, default and `-raw`) returned **60/60** resume items (every bullet, card line, summary, skill line) intact and in order; the visual `-layout` mode interleaved 11 of 18 card lines. Conclusion: reading order is safe for linear text extractors; two-column cards would break any parser that works by position. The stat cards are read *before* the summary and work history, which pollutes the first content a parser sees. Not tested: any real ATS.
